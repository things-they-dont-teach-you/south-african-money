# Lesson 5: Loan sharks and payday loans
<!-- facts: Credit providers must register; Maximum interest rates; Maximum initiation and service fees; Where to get help -->

## By the end, the learner can

- Explain what a mashonisa (loan shark) is, and why borrowing from an unregistered lender is risky.
- Say what a lender may never do: keep your ID, bank card, SASSA card or PIN.
- Check whether a lender is registered with the NCR.
- Work out whether a short-term loan is charging more than the law allows.
- Name safer options to try first, and where to report a lender.

## Key ideas

**Mashonisas, skoppers, loan sharks.** Informal lenders are often quick and close by, and some
are neighbours people trust. But the law treats them like any other lender: anyone who lends
money and charges interest must register with the NCR, and must keep to the legal maximum
interest and fees (`facts.md`). "50% interest for a month" is far above the limit.

**What a lender may never do.** Keep your ID, bank card, SASSA card or PIN, or ask for your
banking login. Doing so is a criminal offence. If a lender has your card, they can empty your
account the day your wage or grant lands, leaving you nothing to live on, so you borrow again.
Ask for your documents back; if they refuse, report them to the police and the NCR (`facts.md`).
Threats or violence are crimes: go to the police.

**Payday and short-term loans** (from registered lenders, often online or by app) are legal but
expensive. The first short-term loan in a calendar year has a higher interest cap than later
ones, and the initiation and service fees make small loans cost a lot (`facts.md`, lesson 3).

**The debt spiral.** Borrowing to pay back another loan means paying fees and interest twice.
If you need to borrow every month just to get through, that's a sign to look at lesson 6.

**Check before you borrow.**
1. Is the lender on the NCR register of credit providers (`facts.md`)? Search by name or NCR
   number.
2. Did you get a written quote with the total you'll repay?
3. Are the interest and fees within the limits?
4. Do they want your card, ID or PIN? Walk away.

**Try first:** talking to the business you owe about a payment arrangement, asking your employer
about a salary advance, a stokvel you belong to (lesson 7), or cutting costs for a month
(lesson 1).

## Worked example

Zanele needs R1 000 before payday. A mashonisa offers it for R1 500 back in a month, and wants
her SASSA card "as security". Using `facts.md`, a first short-term loan of R1 000 for a month may
cost at most R50 interest, a R165 initiation fee and a R60 service fee: R1 275. The mashonisa is
charging more than the law allows, and holding her card is a crime.

## Exercise

Give the learner a made-up offer: R2 000 now, R2 800 back in one month, "leave your bank card
with us". Ask them to list everything wrong with it, using `facts.md`, and say who they could
report it to.
