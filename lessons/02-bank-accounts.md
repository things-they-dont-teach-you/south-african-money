# Lesson 2: Bank accounts and fees
<!-- facts: Deposit insurance; Where to get help -->

## By the end, the learner can

- Say why keeping money in a bank account is usually safer than cash.
- Read a bank statement and find the fees.
- Compare accounts by the fees they would actually pay, not the advertised headline.
- Explain debit orders, and what to do about one they didn't agree to.
- Say what deposit insurance covers, and where to complain about a bank.

## Key ideas

**Why bank at all.** Cash can be stolen or lost and earns nothing. A bank account lets you get
paid, pay accounts by debit order, and build a record that helps when you apply for a lease or
credit later.

**Kinds of account.** A *transaction* (current or cheque) account is for everyday money. A
*savings* account usually pays a little interest and has fewer free transactions. Some banks
offer low-cost or entry-level accounts; compare them.

**Fees are the cost of an account.** There may be a monthly fee, plus fees per transaction: cash
withdrawals (more at another bank's ATM), card swipes, transfers, SMS notifications, a declined
debit order, a cash deposit. Every bank publishes a fee guide. The cheapest account is the one
that fits *how you use it*.

**Ways to cut fees.** Use your own bank's ATMs, or draw cash at till points in shops; pay by card
or app rather than drawing cash; use app notifications instead of paid SMSes; avoid unpaid debit
orders, which carry a fee.

**Read your statement every month.** Check each debit order and fee. A debit order you didn't
agree to should be disputed with your bank straight away; ask it how.

**Your money is protected up to a limit.** If a bank fails, deposit insurance covers qualifying
deposits up to an amount per person per bank (`facts.md`).

**Complaints.** Complain to the bank first, in writing, and keep the reference number. If it
doesn't fix things, the National Financial Ombud Scheme handles bank complaints for free
(`facts.md`).

## Worked example

Sipho's account has a R70 monthly fee. Each month he draws cash four times at another bank's ATM,
gets SMS alerts and has one debit order bounce. Looking at his statement, those extras cost more
than the monthly fee. Switching to his own bank's ATMs, app notifications and moving the debit
order to the day after payday cuts most of them, without changing banks.

## Exercise

Ask the learner to look at a recent statement (theirs, or one you describe) and list every fee
line and what caused it. Remind them not to share account numbers or balances. Then ask which
two fees they could avoid next month, and how.
