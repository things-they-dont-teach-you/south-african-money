# Lesson 7: Saving, emergency funds and stokvels
<!-- facts: Stokvels; Deposit insurance; Tax-free savings accounts -->

## By the end, the learner can

- Explain why an emergency fund matters, and set a first small saving goal.
- Build a saving habit on a small or irregular income.
- Explain how a stokvel works, the main types, and how to protect members' money.
- Say what a tax-free savings account is, and where to learn more.

## Key ideas

**Why save.** Emergencies always come: a broken phone, a funeral, a taxi strike, a month without
work. Without savings, the only option is often an expensive loan (lesson 5). An **emergency
fund** is money kept aside only for these.

**Start small.** The first goal can be modest: enough to cover one unexpected bill. Then build
towards a month of essential costs, then more. What matters is the habit, not the amount.

**Make it automatic.** Save the day money comes in (lesson 1). A separate account, or a savings
"pocket" in your banking app, makes it harder to spend by accident. Keeping it in a bank also
protects it from theft, and bank deposits are insured up to a limit (`facts.md`).

**Stokvels** are groups of people who pool money regularly. Common types:
- **Rotating (savings club):** each month, one member gets the whole pot.
- **Grocery:** money is pooled to buy groceries in bulk, often at year-end.
- **Burial society:** members pay in to help with funeral costs.
- **Investment:** the pool is invested together.

Stokvels add discipline and community support. They work on trust, so good rules matter: a
written constitution, a group bank account needing more than one signature, records of every
payment, and regular reports to members. Stokvels holding more than a set amount must belong to
an approved self-regulatory body (`facts.md`).

**Warning signs:** a "stokvel" that promises fixed high returns, pays out mainly from new
members' money, or pressures you to recruit others may be a pyramid scheme (lesson 8).

**Tax-free savings accounts** let interest and growth build without tax, within yearly and
lifetime limits. The south-african-tax course covers them (lesson 4 there); investing is in the
south-african-investing course.

## Worked example

Ten friends start a rotating stokvel at R500 each a month. Each member gets R5 000 once in ten
months. They open a group bank account needing two signatures, write down the payout order, and
agree in their constitution what happens if someone stops paying. The pot never goes over
R100 000, but they keep records anyway.

## Exercise

Ask the learner to design a simple stokvel: what type, how much each month, and three rules that
protect members' money. Then ask: what's one sign that a "stokvel" might really be a scam?
