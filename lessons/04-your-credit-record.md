# Lesson 4: Your credit record
<!-- facts: Your credit record -->

## By the end, the learner can

- Explain what a credit bureau is and what a credit record shows.
- Say why a credit record matters, even for people who don't want loans.
- Get their free credit report, and know how often it's free (`facts.md`).
- Dispute wrong information, and spot "clear your name for a fee" offers.

## Key ideas

**Credit bureaus** collect information on how people pay their accounts: loans, store cards,
cellphone contracts, some municipal and rental accounts. Lenders, landlords, cellphone companies
and some employers check it. Bureaus are registered with the NCR.

**What's on it.** Accounts you have, whether you pay on time, how much you owe, court judgments,
and a flag if you're under debt review. Many bureaus also give a **credit score**, a number that
sums up how risky you look to a lender.

**Why it matters.** A good record makes it easier and often cheaper to get credit, a phone
contract or a flat to rent. A bad record can mean being refused, or paying higher interest.

**How to build a good one.** Pay every account on time, even if it's the minimum. Don't open
accounts you don't need. Keep balances well below the limit. Paying a small account well over time
builds a record, but borrowing just to build one costs money.

**Check it yourself, for free.** You have a right to a free credit report from each bureau once
in any 12 months (`facts.md`). Request it on the bureau's own website or app.

**Wrong information.** If something is wrong (an account you never opened, a debt you've paid),
complain to the bureau. It has a set time to resolve it (`facts.md`). An account you never opened
can mean identity fraud; report it to your bank and the police too.

**Nobody can sell you a clean record.** If a negative listing is correct, the NCR's advice is to
pay the debt so the information can be removed. You never need to pay a third party to remove
information (`facts.md`). Ads promising to "remove your blacklisting" for a fee are a warning sign.

**If you're refused credit,** you can ask the lender why.

## Worked example

Nomsa is refused a cellphone contract. She asks why, and is told it's her credit record. She
requests her free report and finds a store account listed as unpaid, which she settled last year.
She sends the bureau her paid-up letter and lodges a dispute. The bureau must resolve it within
the time in `facts.md`, and correct the record if she is right.

## Exercise

Ask the learner: how often can you get a free report, and what would you do if you saw an account
you never opened? Then ask them to explain, in their own words, why "pay us R500 to clear your
name" is a red flag.
